# Clinical update: second evidence pass on MPFL reconstruction rehab

Date: 2026-11-08. Scope: rehabilitation after isolated MPFL reconstruction (MPFLR), with notes on combined surgery and adolescents.

This file is research input. It is not medical advice. It changes no plan, skill or code. A surgeon protocol always overrides it.

Labels (same meaning as `form-opus.md`):
- V: I opened the full text or the full protocol and read the numbers there.
- V-abstract: I read only the abstract record (Europe PMC or the journal page).
- V-second-hand: I read the claim in another source that I opened. I did not open the primary source.
- UNVERIFIED: I did not open a page for the claim.
- Population class: MPFLR, MPFLR-COMBINED (with TTO or other bone surgery), BORROWED (ACL, knee arthroscopy, patellofemoral pain, healthy people), OPINION (expert text without data).

Source IDs S1 to S24 are in `clinical.md` section 1. New sources are P1 to P13 (protocols) and N1 to N34 (papers).

## 1. Overview and verdict

The knowledge base is better than before, but the clinical evidence is still thin. The main new facts:

- Thirteen protocol documents are now readable as text (P1 to P13). Eleven cover isolated MPFLR. Four of these documents were UNVERIFIED leads in `clinical.md` section 6. Three of the four old summaries are now confirmed. The "Mayo 2025" lead was a private surgeon (Dr Ben Mayo), not Mayo Clinic.
- Two protocols are evidence-based guidelines with a method: CCHMC (Cincinnati) Guideline 46 (P2, 2015, graded evidence) and NHS Lothian (P3, 2022, systematic search). Both say that most of their content is consensus.
- One international Delphi covers MPFLR rehab (N1, 60 surgeons). It found no consensus on immobilization or weight-bearing. Its rehab statements are second-hand only, because the full text is behind a paywall.
- No AOSSM, ISAKOS, JOSPT or APTA guideline for rehab after MPFLR was found in my search. The ESSKA 2024 consensus (N2) covers first-time dislocation and has no post-operative MPFLR rehab statement.
- The only randomized trial of rehab speed that I found is in combined TTO plus MPFLR (N5, N=50). It is not isolated MPFLR.
- New MPFLR-specific safety data: 30-day DVT 0.3 percent, pulmonary embolism 0.3 percent and surgical site infection 1.3 percent in 617 patients (N11). Pooled patellar fracture 1.17 percent across 110 studies (N9).
- A meta-analysis supports two anatomic risk factors for recurrence after isolated MPFLR (N10). They are patella alta (Caton-Deschamps index more than 1.3) and trochlear dysplasia with a bump (Dejour B or D).
- BFR, NMES, prehabilitation and tele-rehabilitation still have no MPFLR trial. All data are BORROWED from ACL reconstruction or knee surgery.

Verdict: the knowledge base is now good enough to describe the range of practice with sources. It is not good enough to set a default for any patient. Every number that limits load, range, weight or return to sport stays a surgeon decision (`clinical.md` section 9).

Facts on strength recovery:
- N20: Saper MG et al. Orthop J Sports Med 2019. https://doi.org/10.1177/2325967119828953 (V-abstract). Case series, LoE 4, 28 adolescents. At a mean of 7.4 months, quadriceps LSI was 85.3 percent. Only 32 percent passed all 4 hop tests.
- N21: Krych AJ et al. Knee Surg Sports Traumatol Arthrosc 2018. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00167-016-4409-2 (V-abstract). 39 athletes, 41 percent with TTO. At 6 months, the abstract reports flexion and extension strength deficits. The exact extension value was cut off in the record.
- N19: Ryan PC et al. Arthroscopy 2025. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arthro.2024.05.022 (V-abstract). Systematic review, 18 studies, 1072 patients. RTS 60 to 100 percent. Same level 55.6 to 84.0 percent. Fear of re-injury was the top reason for no return in 6 of 12 studies.
- N4: White AE et al. Knee Surg Sports Traumatol Arthrosc 2020. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00167-019-05510-6 (V-abstract). Delphi, LoE 5, 10 US experts, 8-domain RTP checklist. The domains and criteria are behind a paywall.

| P1 HSS | 95 percent of other side, 2.5 to 3.0 Nm/kg | More than 95 percent | Hamstring to quadriceps more than 60 percent |

Corrections to `clinical.md` section 7:
- "No MPFLR VTE study was found" is no longer true. N11 gives 30-day DVT and PE rates for MPFLR.

### 3.3 Other new context

- N7: Straume-Naesheim TM et al. Am J Sports Med 2026. https://doi.org/10.1177/03635465261416931 (V-abstract). RCT, N=61, recurrent dislocation. At 3 years, subjective instability 16.7 percent after MPFLR plus rehab versus 53.6 percent after rehab alone. PROMs did not differ.
- N34: Frodl A et al. Clin Pract 2024. https://doi.org/10.3390/clinpract14060186 (V-abstract). Four patellofemoral experts rated ChatGPT 4.0 answers lower than non-experts did.
- S10 (V) compared ChatGPT, Gemini and Open Evidence. They gave broad principles without procedure-specific detail.

## 6. How good is the knowledge base now (Part D)

Plain answer: the knowledge base describes current practice well and the evidence honestly. The evidence is low or very low for almost every rehab decision after MPFLR. Expert opinion and retrospective data drive most of it. One small RCT studies rehab speed, in combined surgery. One small RCT compares closed with open chain, and only its abstract is available. No validated return-to-sport battery exists. Safety numbers are better than before, but VTE prophylaxis data are borrowed from knee arthroscopy.

A clinician must still decide all items in `clinical.md` section 9. The new evidence does not remove any of them.

Top 10 remaining gaps, ordered by risk to a patient:

1. Stop rules for red flags. Symptom lists are consensus (P2) or general practice. No validated pain or effusion threshold pauses rehab. The app cannot examine effusion. A clinician must set the rules and the contact path.
2. Combined surgery. TTO, trochleoplasty, cartilage work and lateral lengthening change weight-bearing and ROM (P1, P12, `clinical.md` section 3). If the app applies an isolated protocol to combined surgery, the patient can be overloaded.
3. ROM limits in weeks 0 to 6. Protocols range from no limit to a locked 0 to 90 range. Too little motion links to stiffness and manipulation (N22). Too much has no outcome data. A clinician sets the limit.
4. Weight-bearing and brace. N1 found no consensus. Only retrospective data compare options (S7, S8, S9).
5. Depth and load in squat, lunge and leg press. All stress data come from healthy people (S14 to S18, N23). No graft-strain data exist for exercise after MPFLR.
6. Return to running, jumping and sport. Criteria are borrowed from ACL. MPFL-RSI is not validated. Thresholds range from 85 to 95 percent. Many patients do not meet them at 6 to 8 months (S6, N20).
7. VTE prophylaxis. MPFLR data cover 30 days in one registry (N11). Prophylaxis trials are in knee arthroscopy (N12, N13).
8. Anatomic risk. Patella alta and trochlear dysplasia raise recurrence risk after isolated MPFLR (N10). The app does not know the anatomy. The surgeon must flag high-risk patients.
9. Adolescents and bilateral cases. Data are few (N15, N20, N22). LSI is not usable in bilateral instability (N3, OPINION).
10. Webcam measurement in this population. Angle error and margin come from other populations (`form-opus.md` section 3). No study checks a webcam against a goniometer after MPFLR.

Adjuncts (BFR, NMES, tele-rehab, prehab) are lower risk for the app, because the app does not deliver them. They stay surgeon decisions with BORROWED evidence only.
