# Clinical evidence base: rehabilitation after MPFL reconstruction

Date: 2026-11-08. Scope: adult and adolescent isolated MPFL reconstruction (MPFLR).

This file is research input for a webcam exercise tracker. It is not medical advice. It does not give treatment as fact. A surgeon protocol always overrides this file.

## How to read this file

- V means the source was opened in this session and the numbers come from its abstract or text.
- UNVERIFIED means no source was opened for the claim. Treat it as a design proposal only.
- Europe PMC abstracts were used where PubMed did not load. Full texts were not read, except Hsu 2025 (first 100,000 characters).
- Level of evidence (LoE) is given only when the source states it. Otherwise the design is named.
- Institution protocol PDFs (Dartmouth, Lahey, Mayo, UVA, Bankston) did not yield readable text. Their content is UNVERIFIED here (see section 6).

## 2. Phase-based protocol (evidence summary, not a prescription)

No validated protocol exists. S1 says this directly. S2 found 1 RCT among 85 studies. All week ranges below are ranges from the literature, not targets for a patient.

The main reference frame is S1 (LoE 5) because it is the only source with phase detail that was read. Other sources add the points of disagreement.

### Phase 1: Protection, weeks 0 to 6 (S1)

- Goals (S1): reduce pain and swelling, regain full extension, regain flexion, control the quadriceps (straight leg raise, SLR, without lag).
- ROM (S1): begin within 2 weeks. A typical start is 0 to 30 degrees in weeks 1 to 2. Progress by 20 to 30 degrees each week. Full flexion is reported at 4 to 12 weeks.
- Weight-bearing (S1): usually not limited for isolated MPFLR. Crutches help at first. It can be limited after TTO or a cartilage procedure.
- Brace (S1): a hinged brace is common. Duration across protocols is 0 to 12 weeks.
- Exercises (S1): heel slides, wall slides, heel props, prone hangs, quad sets, 3-way SLR, isometric knee extension and flexion, bridges, clamshells, weight shifts, balance.
- Exit criteria (S1): flexion more than 110 degrees, full extension, SLR without lag, walking without crutches.

### Phase 2: Strengthening, weeks 6 to 12 (S1)

- Goals: full ROM, normal gait, strength.
- Exercises: progressive lower-body strengthening at RPE 6 to 8 of 10, balance and proprioception (wobble board, perturbations), careful single-leg work, cycling, rowing.
- Exit criteria: full ROM, quadriceps more than 60 percent of the other side, normal gait.
- Run start (S1) needs three items. Full ROM or more than 95 percent of the other knee. Pain below 2 of 10. Quadriceps limb symmetry index (LSI) more than 70 percent (isometric).
- S1 advises to consult the surgeon if arthrofibrosis is a concern.

### Phase 3: Advanced strengthening and function, weeks 12 to 16 (S1)

- Exercises: leg press, squat, deadlift, lunges, step-ups, step-downs, split squats. Loads above 80 percent of one-repetition maximum are possible. Eccentric and power work.
- Early plyometrics and change of direction. S1 cites other literature for plyometric readiness: 30-second single-leg stance with eyes open and closed, and 20 percent or less strength deficit. This criterion is not from MPFLR studies (UNVERIFIED for MPFLR).
- Hop tests are recommended at 12 to 19 weeks (S1).
- Exit criteria (S1): quadriceps more than 80 percent LSI, hops more than 80 percent LSI.

### Phase 4: Return to sport, after week 16 or 20 (S1)

- S1 gives more than 16 weeks in its table and more than 20 weeks in its text. S1 does not resolve this.
- Content (S1): reactive agility, single-leg and explosive plyometrics, sport-specific pivoting and cutting, dual-task drills. Foot contacts of 80 to 100 per session for novices and 120 to 140 for advanced patients. Progress from non-contact to contact to competition.

### Return-to-sport (RTS) criteria

- S1 proposes: quadriceps LSI more than 90 percent. Hop LSI more than 90 percent. Y-Balance composite more than 90 percent with anterior reach difference below 4 cm. Kujala, IKDC and KOOS scores. MPFL-RSI above 56.
- These criteria borrow from ACL practice. S1 states that MPFLR has no consensus.
- S3: only 10 of 53 studies (18.9 percent) used objective or subjective criteria. Most used time.
- S6: 6 studies, tests at 6 to 8 months. Isokinetic extension LSI was 70 to 96 percent. Only 14 to 40 percent of patients met all study criteria, where reported. Quadriceps often stayed under 90 percent LSI at RTS.
- S10: RTS criteria were time, function or strength based in 76 to 89 percent of protocols. Psychological readiness appeared in 43 percent. Physician approval appeared in 22 percent.
- S21 (LoE 3): in 97 adolescents, an ACL-RSI cutoff of 59.2 or more predicted RTS (OR 3.56, 95% CI 1.10 to 11.51). The AUC was only 0.70. ACL-RSI is not validated for MPFLR in this source.

### RTS timing and outcomes

- S4 (27 studies, 1278 patients): RTP 85.1 percent. 68.3 percent returned to the same level. Mean time 7.0 months. Recurrent instability 5.4 percent.
- S5 (23 studies, 930 patients): RTS 92.8 percent. 71.3 percent returned to or above the previous level. Mean 6.7 months. Recurrent instability 1.9 percent.
- S2: RTS was most often set at 6 months (35 programmes).
- S1: average RTS at 7 to 8 months. Rates 53 to 91 percent.

## 4. Exercise list and joint angles for a webcam tracker

A 2D webcam gives reliable sagittal-plane knee flexion in a side view. Hip and ankle angles are less reliable. Frontal-plane knee valgus is possible from a front view but has no validated threshold here.

Knee flexion is the angle at the knee between thigh and shank. 0 degrees means full extension.

### Evidence on loading by knee angle (healthy subjects, not MPFLR patients)

- S14 (N=20): leg extension loads the patellofemoral joint more at 0 and 30 degrees. Leg press loads it more at 60 and 90 degrees. The curves cross at 48 degrees.
- S15 (N=15): squat patellofemoral force and stress rise with flexion. Peak at 90 degrees. Loaded squats raise stress at 45 to 90 degrees in descent.
- S16 (N=18): wall squat and one-leg squat differ at 60 to 90 degrees. The authors suggest 0 to 50 degrees can limit loading.
- S17 (N=18): lunge stress rises with flexion. A short step gives more stress at 70 to 90 degrees than a long step. A stride adds stress at 10 to 40 degrees.
- S18 (N=20): at 45 degrees of knee flexion, step-down gave higher stress (13.8 MPa) than step-up (11.2 MPa) or lateral step-up (11.5 MPa).
- These studies are on healthy people. Their link to MPFL graft safety or outcome is UNVERIFIED.
- S1 (LoE 5): in closed-chain work, avoid femoral internal rotation with valgus. S1 gives no angle limits.

### Measurable quantities for the app

- Knee flexion peak and range for each rep. Source S1 uses ROM milestones (30, 90, 110 degrees and full).
- Full extension at the end of SLR and quad set. S1 uses SLR without lag as a criterion.
- Left and right comparison of the same quantity. S1 uses LSI of 70, 80 and 90 percent. A webcam cannot measure strength, so the app can only compare ROM and movement symmetry. Do not label this LSI.
- Knee valgus in squat, step and landing. S1 says avoid it. No numeric threshold was found. Any threshold is UNVERIFIED.
- Pain score (0 to 10) from user input. S1 uses pain below 2 of 10 for return to run.
- Ankle angle and hip angle: no source gives a target for MPFLR. Use them only as quality cues.

## 5. Blood flow restriction (BFR), neuromuscular training, and other adjuncts

- BFR in MPFLR: no MPFLR study was found. S1 does not mention BFR in the part read.
- BFR in patellofemoral pain (S19, RCT, N=79, 69 completed): 8 weeks of leg press and leg extension. BFR used 30 percent 1RM. Standard used 70 percent 1RM. BFR gave 93 percent greater pain reduction in daily activities (p=0.02). Kujala, worst pain and torque did not differ overall. No difference at 6 months.
- S19 is not a post-surgical population. Transfer to MPFLR is UNVERIFIED.
- S20 (clinical review): BFR is a bridge when high loads are not tolerated. It covers safety and application. Details of contraindications were not extracted (UNVERIFIED).
- Neuromuscular training (S1): balance, perturbation, wobble board, dual-task and reactive drills are listed. S1 gives no trial data. LoE 5.
- NMES (S1): "considered" in phase 1. No dose or trial data was read.
- Psychological readiness: S1 lists MPFL-RSI above 56 as readiness. S21 supports readiness as a predictor in adolescents.

## 7. Contraindications, red flags and safety alerts

Most items here come from general clinical practice and not from a source opened in this session. Mark them UNVERIFIED in the app text. The app must route every alert to the surgeon or physician.

### Supported by a verified source

- Recurrent instability is a known failure. Rates: 1.2 percent (S11, 14 studies), 1.9 percent (S5), 5.4 percent (S4), 7.27 percent braced and 1.56 percent unbraced (S8). Apprehension sign in 3.6 percent (S11).
- Stiffness with loss of knee flexion is a known complication. S13 (LoE 4): 8 of 179 knees had major stiffness. S12 lists loss of flexion.
- Patellar fracture is a known complication. S13: 6 of 179 knees. S12 lists it.
- Overall complication rates differ by study: 26.1 percent in 629 knees (S12), 16.2 percent in 179 knees (S13), 8.8 percent (S5). Definitions differ.
- S13: 47 percent of complications were judged technical and preventable. Female sex and bilateral surgery were risk factors.
- Wound complications and pain are listed in S12.
- S1: consult the surgeon if arthrofibrosis is a concern. S1: caution with femoral internal rotation and valgus in closed-chain work.
- S23 (LoE IV, N=23): after trochleoplasty plus MPFLR, quadriceps and hamstring strength stayed reduced at 2 years. Patellofemoral cartilage worsened in 18 of 24 knees. The app must not apply an isolated-MPFLR protocol to combined surgery.

### Not verified (general practice, no source opened)

- Redislocation signs: a new "giving way" feeling, a visible patella shift, a sudden pop, sudden swelling, an inability to bear weight. UNVERIFIED.
- Deep vein thrombosis (DVT): calf pain, calf swelling, warmth, redness. UNVERIFIED for MPFLR. No MPFLR VTE study was found.
- Pulmonary embolism: sudden shortness of breath, chest pain. UNVERIFIED.
- Infection: fever, spreading redness, wound drainage, increasing wound pain. UNVERIFIED.
- Effusion: large or increasing swelling after exercise. UNVERIFIED. No numeric threshold found.
- Pain threshold: S1 uses pain below 2 of 10 for return to run. No source gives a stop threshold for other exercises. UNVERIFIED.
- Numbness or loss of foot pulse. UNVERIFIED.

### Suggested app behavior (PROPOSAL)

- Show a stop-and-call message for any red flag above. Give no diagnosis.
- Block progression to a new phase until the user reports surgeon clearance.
- Log pain and swelling for each session and show the trend to the surgeon.

## 8. Evidence quality and uncertainty

| Claim | Evidence | Quality note |
|-------|----------|--------------|
| Weight-bearing as tolerated is acceptable after isolated MPFLR | S1 (LoE 5), S7 (retrospective, 123 knees), S10 (structured review) | Low. No RCT. Retrospective data. |
| Accelerated rehab does not raise redislocation | S7: 3 events (2.4 percent), all in the restrictive group | Low. Few events. Era effects. |
| No brace is as safe as a brace | S8 (LoE III, 229 knees), S9 (7 studies, p = 0.055) | Low. Non-random. Reoperation signal in S8 is possibly confounded. |
| Time to RTS is about 6.7 to 7 months | S4, S5 (meta-analyses) | Moderate for time. Studies are case series. |
| RTS rate is 85 to 93 percent | S4, S5 | Moderate. Only 68 to 71 percent return to the prior level. |
| Quadriceps LSI of 90 percent is the RTS goal | S1 (LoE 5) | Very low. Borrowed from ACL. S6 shows many patients do not reach it. |
| MPFL-RSI above 56 shows readiness | S1 (cited from other work) | Very low. Primary source not opened. |
| Psychological readiness predicts RTS | S21 (LoE 3, N=97, AUC 0.70) | Low. Adolescents. ACL-RSI scale. |
| Knee angle changes patellofemoral stress | S14 to S18 (lab, N=15 to 20) | Moderate for healthy people. Unknown for MPFLR grafts. |
| BFR helps after MPFLR | None | No evidence. |
| Hip and ankle targets | None | No evidence. |

Open clinical questions:

- What ROM limit is safe in weeks 0 to 6? No trial answers this.
- Is a brace useful for any patient group?
- Which RTS criteria predict reinjury after MPFLR?
- Do concurrent procedures (TTO, trochleoplasty) change the loading limits? S10 and S2 say yes. No dose data was found.
- Does graft type or fixation change the protocol? S22 compares two grafts in 35 patients and recommends personalised protocols. Evidence is low.
- Do webcam angle measures agree with goniometer values? UNVERIFIED. No validation source was opened.
- DVT risk and prophylaxis after MPFLR are not covered by any source opened here.

## 9. What the app must leave to the surgeon

The app must show these items as "ask your surgeon". It must not set a default.

1. The start date and the daily limit of knee flexion in weeks 0 to 6.
2. Weight-bearing status and crutch use. TTO or cartilage work changes this (S1, S10).
3. Brace use: yes or no, locked or open, and the end date. Sources disagree (section 3).
4. Any change in protocol for concurrent surgery: TTO, trochleoplasty, cartilage repair, lateral release or medial tibial tubercle transfer.
5. Graft type, fixation and tunnel details that limit loading or hamstring work.
6. Permission to start each new phase. Section 2 criteria are a reference only.
7. Maximum squat or lunge depth and the load limit, because stress rises with flexion (S14 to S17).
8. Permission to run, jump, cut and pivot.
9. Final RTS clearance and the criteria used. No consensus exists (S1, S3, S6).
10. Use of BFR, NMES or other devices. No MPFLR evidence exists (section 5).
11. Handling of red flags: redislocation, DVT, infection, effusion, stiffness. The app can only tell the user to contact a clinician.
12. Anticoagulation or thromboprophylaxis. No source was opened.
13. Changes for skeletal immaturity, bilateral surgery, revision surgery, or high-risk anatomy. S13 lists female sex and bilateral surgery as risk factors. S24 (LoE III, 51 patients) found similar outcomes with borderline anatomy, but the sample is small.
14. Pain and swelling thresholds that pause the program.
