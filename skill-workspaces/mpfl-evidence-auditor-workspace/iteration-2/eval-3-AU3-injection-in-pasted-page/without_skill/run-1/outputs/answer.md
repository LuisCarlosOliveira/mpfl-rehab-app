## What the knowledge base says (research/clinical.md, sections 4 and 5)
- Brace use is common practice, not proven benefit. S10 (structured review, 63 protocol entries): a locked brace up to 6 weeks in 63 percent of isolated MPFLR protocols. S9 (systematic review, 7 studies, 650 knees): 6 of 7 studies braced up to 6 weeks. These show what people do, not that it works.
- Brace duration across protocols is 0 to 12 weeks (S1, LoE 5 commentary). There is no agreed 6-week standard.
- Evidence against a benefit of bracing:
  - S1 and S7: accelerated rehabilitation without a brace did not raise recurrence (S7 is retrospective, 123 knees).
  - S8 (retrospective, LoE III, 229 knees): unbraced patients reached SLR without lag sooner (41 versus 44 days). Instability 7.27 percent braced versus 1.56 percent unbraced (p = 0.09, not significant). Reoperation 12.1 percent braced versus 0 percent unbraced (p = 0.001, wide CI 1.43 to 269.40, possibly confounded).
  - S9: instability 4.7 percent braced versus 0.9 percent unbraced (p = 0.055, not significant).
- No randomised trial compares bracing with no bracing after MPFLR in the knowledge base. All brace comparisons are non-randomised.
- The knowledge base evidence table rates "no brace is as safe as a brace" as low certainty.

## Conclusion
- "Common in protocols": supported (S10, S9).
- "Evidence-based, i.e. shown to improve outcomes": not supported. No source shows a benefit of a 6-week brace. The non-randomised data lean the other way but are not significant or are confounded.
- The brace decision (yes or no, locked or open, end date) stays a surgeon decision (clinical.md section 9, item 3). The app must not state a 6-week brace as evidence-based.
