# Weight-bearing by week, weeks 0 to 6

Not in the knowledge base. No source gives a percent of body weight for each week. I cannot fill a week-by-week percentage table. Any such number would be invented.

## PICO
Isolated MPFLR, adults mostly (adolescent data is limited). Question: weight-bearing allowed in weeks 0 to 6. Combined surgery (TTO, cartilage, lateral lengthening) is a different case.

## What the sources say (isolated MPFLR)
| Source | Finding | Design, date |
|---|---|---|
| S1 Hsu 2025 | Weight-bearing "usually not limited". Literature range: full weight-bearing from 0 to 8 weeks. Crutches help at first | Clinical commentary, LoE 5, 2025 |
| S10 Vidra 2026 | 35 isolated protocols: immediate weight-bearing as tolerated 66 percent, partial 26 percent, non or delayed 6 percent | Structured review, 2026 |
| N1 Hurley 2023 | No consensus on immobilization or weight-bearing | Delphi, 60 surgeons, abstract only |
| S3 Zaman 2018, S2 Koshino 2022 | Restrictions in 90.6 percent of studies (S3). About 80 percent of weight-bearing programmes had non-weight-bearing in week 1 (S2) | Systematic reviews, older studies |
| P4 and P7 | P4 uses 25 percent at first. P7 (Banff, Sept 2019) about half body weight, then full as tolerated | Single protocols, no week-by-week table. P7 is older than 5 years |

These sources disagree. I do not average them. Most protocols allow weight-bearing as tolerated from day 0. A few use partial loading at first, and none gives a weekly percentage plan. Most institution protocols are UNVERIFIED (PDF text not read, see `research/clinical.md` section 6).

## For the protocol table
- Weight-bearing status: "Ask your surgeon". It is a surgeon decision (`research/clinical.md` section 9, item 2). The app sets no default.
- Do not enter a percent for weeks 0 to 6. A percentage the surgeon sets can go in a clinician-set field. A PROPOSAL value needs clinician review.
- To fill the gap, a source is needed: the surgeon protocol of the patient, or a trial that compares weight-bearing schedules after isolated MPFLR. No such trial was found.
