PICO: Surgery type not stated. This answer covers isolated MPFL reconstruction (ISO). Age group not stated. Week 7 after surgery. Question: can the app move the patient to phase 3?

## Answer

I cannot move the patient to phase 3. The app never changes the phase. A surgeon decides when a patient starts a new phase (`research/clinical.md` section 9, item 6). The criteria in the knowledge base are a reference only.

The two findings do not decide the phase. The numbers also do not match a phase 3 start in the sources.

Do this instead:
- Send the two findings to the surgeon or the physiotherapist.
- Let the surgeon or physiotherapist change the phase in the app, if the app has a clinician field for it.
- If the surgery was combined (TTO, trochleoplasty, cartilage procedure), a surgeon protocol is required. Isolated MPFLR rules do not apply.

## What the two findings do and do not show

- Flexion of 125 degrees is above the S1 phase 1 exit value (more than 110 degrees). It is above the P2 value for week 6 (120 degrees). It is also in the range of N3 (2024), which gives passive flexion of more than 125 degrees in weeks 0 to 6 (`citation-audit.md` M1, VERIFIED, ISO).
- It is not stated if the 125 degrees is passive or active, or if a goniometer or a camera gave it. The sources use passive range. The app camera measures active motion, with an error of about 9 degrees at best (`citation-audit.md` V85). An app angle is an estimate, not a medical measurement.
- SLR without lag matches the S1 phase 1 exit item. P2 allows up to 10 degrees of lag. A lag below the 2D error is not measurable by camera. P1 uses SLR without lag to decide brace use. A false "no lag" from the app can lead to early walking without a brace (`citation-audit.md` section 4.2).
- These two items are phase 1 exit items. They are not phase 3 criteria.
- Phase 2 exit has more items: full ROM, quadriceps strength above 60 percent of the other side, and normal gait. Strength and gait are clinician inputs. The app cannot measure them (`rehab-rules.md` section 5).
- Phase 3 starts at 12 weeks in S1 (window 12 to 16 weeks) and at about 12 weeks in P3. The patient is at week 7. A move to phase 3 is a move of about 5 weeks ahead of the source windows. Sources say the weeks are approximate and criteria guide progress. The knowledge base has no source that supports skipping phase 2.
- No study compares criteria-based with time-based progression after MPFLR. No trial shows that algorithmic phase change helps. Class NONE for outcome evidence (`rehab-rules.md` section 5, `progression.md` section 2.2).

## Disagreements

- Phase 3 start: S1 gives 12 to 16 weeks. P3 gives about 12 weeks. P2 gives no weeks. The conservative value is the later one, 12 weeks or more, with surgeon clearance. Values are not averaged.
- Flexion: P2 gives 120 degrees by week 6. S1 gives more than 110 degrees at the phase 1 exit. N3 gives more than 125 degrees in weeks 0 to 6. Flexion has two risks: too much motion can strain the graft after a bone procedure, and too little can lead to stiffness (S1, N22). The surgeon sets the value.

## Ask your surgeon

- Is it time to start phase 3, and which criteria do you use?
- What is the daily limit of knee flexion? The surgeon sets it (`clinical.md` section 9, item 1).
- May the patient change the weight-bearing status, the brace, or the load? (items 2, 3, 7)
- Is the surgery combined with a bone or cartilage procedure?

## Gaps

- Phase 3 start by week: no source gives a number for week 7. NONE. The literature gives 12 to 16 weeks (S1) and about 12 weeks (P3).
- Validated protocol after MPFLR: NONE. S1: "no validated rehabilitation protocols are established following MPFL reconstruction".
- A strength percentage, a gait result and an effusion grade for this patient: not given. The app cannot measure them.
- Whether the 125 degrees is active or passive, and how it was measured: not given.
