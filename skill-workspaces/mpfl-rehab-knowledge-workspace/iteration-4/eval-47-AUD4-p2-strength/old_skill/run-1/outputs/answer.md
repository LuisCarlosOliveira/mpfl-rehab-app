# Is P2 a strong consensus guideline that the app can use as fixed limits?

Short answer: no. P2 is a real, graded guideline that the knowledge base read in full. But its strength is weak, and the app must not use its numbers as fixed limits. A surgeon protocol overrides it.

## What P2 is

- P2: Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center, Evidence-Based Care Guideline 46, "Post-Operative Management of Lateral Patellar Dislocations and Instability", ages 8 to 25, January 2015 (`research/rehab-rules.md` source table; `research/clinical-update.md` section 3).
- Access: VERIFIED-FULL (PDF text, 38 pages, `research/source-audit.md`). Quotes are EXACT in the citation audit (V11, V39, V45, V49, V52, V55 and others).
- Population: MPFL repair, reconstruction or revision, ages 8 to 25. It excludes tibial tubercle re-alignment and lateral retinacular release (V33). So it does not cover combined surgery. It mixes adolescents and young adults, and it does not match an adult-only case.
- Date: January 2015. This is more than 5 years old. P2 says its team reconvenes after five years. The knowledge base found no revision. A newer version needs a search.

## Why it is not strong

1. P2 grades almost all of its content as evidence level 5. Level 5 means "General review, expert opinion, case report, consensus report, or guideline". The items cite "LocalConcensus 2014 [5]" (`research/citation-audit.md`, lines 136 to 140).
2. All 55 recommendations use "It is recommended". P2 defines this as "There is consensus that benefits are closely balanced with risks and burdens". No item is "Strongly recommended".
3. The citation audit changed the mark. The earlier mark CONSENSUS (low) overstated it. In substance, the P2 rehab items are OPINION of one institution team, weak strength, 2015. The audit says: "Reference value for the clinician. Never a fixed limit".
4. Only a few items carry better grades. For example, the red flag item (rec 4) is "level 5 and 1b for effusion", and two items have a 1b review. Rec 26 cites one level 2b source. None of these makes a fixed limit.

## The numbers are the wrong kind of value for a limit

- The flexion goals are passive range (PROM): "at least 90 degrees knee flexion PROM by 4 weeks" and "at least 120 degrees by 6 weeks" (V11). A camera measures active motion, and the webcam angle error is about 9 degrees (best case). They are not the same quantity. They are phase goals, not ceilings. The app must never use them as a "bend further" push or a ceiling. They are reference values for the clinician.
- The pain value, 2 out of 10 on the numeric rating scale, is a phase goal for walking and daily tasks. It is not a stop rule (V52).
- The straight leg raise lag (10 degrees or less) and the squat goals (90 degrees double leg, 60 degrees single) are phase goals at level 5 (V39, V42).
- The dose, 2 sets of 10 to 15 reps then 3 sets, has no adult or MPFLR study behind it (rec 26). The generator design gives no dose because of this.
- Hop LSI at least 85 and the other criteria differ from other sources: S1 gives at least 90, others give 95 (`rehab-rules.md`, "Thresholds in use"). The sources disagree (85 to 95 percent). I do not average them.

## What the app can do with P2

- Cite it as a reference value or a pattern, with the mark: institution guideline, level 5, 2015, ages 8 to 25, not a fixed limit.
- Use the P2 red-flag list (rec 4) as a "notify the physician" trigger. It is the only post-operative MPFLR stop logic in a guideline. Over-referral costs little (`citation-audit.md` line 149).
- The knee-bend limit, the phase start and the dose stay with the surgeon (`research/clinical.md` section 9, items 1, 6 and 14: ask your surgeon). The app sets no default.
- Any value that the app picks itself is a PROPOSAL and needs clinician review.
