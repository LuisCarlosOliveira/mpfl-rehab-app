Not in the knowledge base. The knowledge base has no rehab rules for revision MPFLR.

**PICO restatement**
- Surgery type: revision MPFLR (a repeat operation after a failed first MPFLR).
- Age group: not given.
- Weeks since surgery: not given.
- Outcome: which rehab rules apply.

**What the knowledge base covers and what it does not**
- Its scope is isolated MPFLR, adult and adolescent (`research/clinical.md` header). It also notes combined surgery (`research/clinical-update.md`). I found no source about revision surgery, so I give no phase, angle, week, load or weight-bearing value for it. Any value would come from memory or from isolated MPFLR, and the isolated-MPFLR numbers are not shown to apply to revision.
- The only mention is a surgeon decision. Changes for skeletal immaturity, bilateral surgery, revision surgery or high-risk anatomy are surgeon decisions: ask your surgeon (`research/clinical.md` section 9, item 13). The app must show this as "ask your surgeon" and must not set a default.
- Related facts that are not about revision:
  - Recurrent instability is a known failure. Rates are 1.2 percent (S11), 1.9 percent (S5), 5.4 percent (S4) and 7.27 percent braced versus 1.56 percent unbraced (S8, non-randomised, not significant) (`research/clinical.md` section 7 and section 3). These rates are for first MPFLR and do not describe revision.
  - S13 found that 47 percent of complications were judged technical and preventable. Female sex and bilateral surgery were risk factors (`research/clinical.md` section 7). This is not a revision study.
  - S23 (LoE IV, N=23) is for trochleoplasty plus MPFLR, not revision. It warns that the app must not apply an isolated-MPFLR protocol to combined surgery (`research/clinical.md` section 7). The same caution is a reasonable reading for revision, but that is a PROPOSAL and needs clinician review. No source states it for revision.
  - Limb symmetry index (LSI) is called inappropriate in bilateral instability (N3, OPINION) (`research/clinical-update.md` section 4). It is not a revision rule.

**What to do**
- The surgeon or the physiotherapist under the surgeon sets the full protocol for a revision patient.
- To add this to the knowledge base, a source on rehab after revision MPFLR is needed, such as a published protocol or a systematic review of revision outcomes. Please give a link. I will mark it UNVERIFIED until the source is opened.
- If the patient has a symptom now, such as pain, swelling or the knee giving way, contact your care team now.
