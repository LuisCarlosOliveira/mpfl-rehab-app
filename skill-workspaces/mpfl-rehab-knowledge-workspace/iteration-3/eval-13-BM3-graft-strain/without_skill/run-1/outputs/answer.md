No. The project knowledge base found no study that measured graft strain during exercise after MPFL reconstruction (MPFLR). The search covered the sources in `research/clinical.md` and `research/clinical-update.md`. This is a result of that search. It does not prove that no such study exists.

What the sources say about graft loading:

- Statements about graft loading in rehab protocols are opinion. Lahey (P5) and Banff (P7) say that the MPFL restrains the patella in the first 20 to 30 degrees of knee flexion. Banff says that flexion beyond 30 to 45 degrees does not strain the graft. P7 cites no data for this.
- The load data that exist come from healthy people, not from patients with a graft. These are S14 (N=20), S15 (N=15), S16 (N=18), S17 (N=18), S18 (N=20) and N23 (N=10). They measure patellofemoral stress, not graft strain.
  - Leg extension loads the patellofemoral joint more at 0 to 30 degrees. Leg press and squat load it more at 60 to 90 degrees (S14, N23).
  - Squat stress peaks at about 90 degrees (S15).
  - Lunge stress rises with flexion (S17).
  - At 45 degrees, step-down gives more stress than step-up (S18).
- N23 and S14 agree with each other. The result is moderate evidence for healthy people. For MPFLR grafts it is unknown.

What this means for the app:

- The app cannot give a graft strain number, and it does not make one up.
- Depth and load limits for squat, lunge and leg press are a surgeon decision. The app can use the healthy-people stress data only as borrowed, labelled evidence.
- The project design documents list "graft strain value" as something the app must not invent.
