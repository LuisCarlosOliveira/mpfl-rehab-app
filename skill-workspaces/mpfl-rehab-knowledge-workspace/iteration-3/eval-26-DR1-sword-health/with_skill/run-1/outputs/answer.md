**PICO:** Isolated MPFL reconstruction is the case of the app. Age group and weeks since surgery are not stated. Question: did Sword Health prove camera feedback after knee surgery?

**Answer:** No. The knowledge base holds no such proof. Sword Health used body-worn sensors, not a camera. The study was not randomized. It was in knee replacement (TKA), not MPFL surgery. No trial of camera feedback after MPFL reconstruction exists (NONE).

**Disagreements:** None found. No source says that camera feedback is proven after knee surgery.

**What the evidence does not show:**
- It does not show that an algorithm that changes the plan helps. Phase change stays a surgeon decision.
- It does not show that a camera cue improves outcome or safety. A camera cue is an app behavior that needs clinician approval. It is not an evidence-based treatment (`research/form-evidence.md`).
- A webcam knee angle has a best-case error of about 9 degrees (`research/form-evidence.md`). It is not a measurement.

**Ask your surgeon or physiotherapist:**
- Whether a digital or camera tool fits your rehab plan.
- Who reviews the data from such a tool.

**Gaps:**
- Camera feedback after MPFL reconstruction: NONE. The literature gives sensor-based feasibility data after TKA (T1), a TKA tele-rehab review with no clear difference (T3), and borrowed camera data from osteoarthritis (Biebl 2021) with no outcome benefit.
- Adaptive progression after MPFL reconstruction: NONE.
- Sources T1, T3, Willy 2019 and Biebl 2021 were not checked in `research/citation-audit.md`.
