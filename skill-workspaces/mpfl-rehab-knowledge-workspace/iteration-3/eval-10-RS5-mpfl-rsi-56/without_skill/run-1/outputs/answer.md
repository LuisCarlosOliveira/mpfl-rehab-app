No. An MPFL-RSI score above 56 is not a validated cutoff.

- The "above 56" figure comes from a 2025 review of rehab after MPFL reconstruction (S1, Hsu et al.). It says "A MPFL-RSI score > 56 points indicates a patient is psychologically ready". This is expert opinion. The review cites other work (N14) for it. The project did not open the primary source, so the evidence grade is very low.
- The MPFL-RSI is an adaptation of the ACL-RSI, with "MPFL" in the text. I found no formal validation of the MPFL-RSI itself. The 56 cutoff comes from ACL work.
- The cohort that is cited for it (N14, Hurley et al. 2021) shows weak separation. 35 of 131 patients did not return to sport, and only 9 of those 35 passed 56.
- The closest related data do not give a validated MPFL cutoff:
  - An ACL-RSI cutoff of 59.2 or more predicted return to sport in 97 adolescents (OR 3.56, 95% CI 1.10 to 11.51). The AUC was only 0.70, and the group mixed ISO and TTO patients (S21).
  - The ACL-RSI had good internal consistency in 32 adolescents after MPFL reconstruction (alpha 0.94). That study gave no cutoff and no predictive validity (N15, Matsuzaki 2026).
- No guideline for rehab after MPFL reconstruction exists.

How to use it: treat 56 as a rough, unvalidated reference for a conversation with the surgeon or physiotherapist. Do not use it as a pass or fail gate for return to sport. The app does not use it as a gate. The return-to-sport decision belongs to the clinician.
