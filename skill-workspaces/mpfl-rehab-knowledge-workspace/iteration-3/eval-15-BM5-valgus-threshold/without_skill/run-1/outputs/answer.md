No. I found no validated valgus threshold for the mini squat after MPFL reconstruction (MPFLR). I will not give you a number.

**What the sources say**
- The main guideline (S1) says closed-chain exercises, such as the mini squat, must be watched. The aim is to prevent femoral internal rotation with dynamic knee valgus. It gives no angle. This is expert opinion (level of evidence 5), not a measured limit.
- Another guideline (P2, rec 40) asks for focus on neuromuscular control of the pelvis, femur, tibia and patella. It also gives no number.
- Two independent searches of the form literature (`research/form.md` and `research/form-opus.md`) found no study that gives a valgus, trunk lean or pelvic drop threshold after MPFLR. No study links a movement pattern to recurrence.

**Numbers that exist, and why they do not apply**
- Some cut-offs come from patellofemoral pain or ACL work. One example is a knee frontal plane projection angle of 8.2 degrees in a single-leg squat. It comes from 16 women with patellofemoral pain and 16 controls. It was not validated on new data, and it does not cover instability or MPFLR.
- Another source reports a video classification at more than 10 degrees valgus. It came from 30 athletes. It is also borrowed and unvalidated.
- Even in ACL research, valgus did not predict injury in 710 athletes (Krosshaug 2016).
- Valgus does matter in the dislocation mechanism. In 10 non-contact dislocation videos, the knee was flexed under a valgus force. This is why clinicians watch it. It does not prove that valgus in a rehab squat causes a new dislocation.

**Why the app cannot measure it either**
- A phone camera gives a large error for the absolute valgus angle. With MediaPipe, the bias is about 19 degrees. This is far larger than any cut-off above.
- The change from the start of a rep is more reliable (bias about 0.18 degrees). But that study had 15 healthy men and a drop landing. Test-retest reliability of the change was only 0.14 to 0.70.
- A meta-analysis found no correlation between 2D and 3D frontal knee angles in the single-leg squat (r = 0.127). The patellofemoral pain guideline gives a Grade B recommendation against visual biofeedback on lower-limb alignment.

**What the app does**
- The app does not use a valgus threshold, a live valgus cue or a "good form" label (plan items FR20 and design item D17: do not build).
- At most, it can record a front-view change value for your clinician (FR24). It does not judge that value.

**What to do**
- If the knee drifts inward in your mini squat, tell your surgeon or physiotherapist. They can set a limit for you.
- Use the depth and flexion limits that your surgeon gave. Those are surgeon decisions, not app decisions.
