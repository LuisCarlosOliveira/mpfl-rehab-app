No. The project knowledge base finds no validated return-to-sport (RTS) test battery after MPFL reconstruction (MPFLR). Criteria exist, but they are consensus, expert opinion or borrowed from ACL work.

**What the sources say**
- A 2025 clinical commentary (Hsu, S1) states that RTS criteria after MPFLR "have not been well established". A 2020 Delphi study (White, N4) found "no consensus for the appropriate timing or functional evaluation".
- Only 10 of 53 studies (18.9%) in a return-to-play systematic review (Zaman, S3) used any criteria.
- For bony realignment surgery, a systematic review (Chatterji, R5) found that all papers lacked adequate return-to-play guidelines.
- No validated rehab protocol exists either. A systematic review of 85 studies (Koshino, S2) found only one randomized trial on rehab.

**What exists, with its status**
- ISAKOS 2013 consensus criteria (read second-hand through Menetrey 2014). They list no pain or instability, near full range of motion, no effusion, hop-test limb symmetry index (LSI) above 85%, and psychological readiness (SANE above 80/100). This is a committee consensus, not a validated battery.
- Menetrey's six criteria: no pain, no effusion, no instability, full range of motion, near-symmetrical strength (85 to 90%) and excellent dynamic stability. This is review-level opinion.
- Hop tests, strength LSI and Y-Balance are borrowed from ACL rehab. No MPFLR study links passing them to lower re-injury (S6). The LSI is "inappropriate" when both knees are unstable (N3, opinion).
- Thresholds differ between sources, from 85 to 95%. The conservative reading is 90% or more for strength and hops (S1, Menetrey). Do not average them.
- MPFL-RSI (pass above 56) is not validated. It is the ACL-RSI with "MPFL" in the text, and the cutoff comes from ACL work. In one cohort, only 9 of 35 patients who did not return to sport passed 56 (Hurley, N14). The ACL-RSI has internal consistency in 32 adolescents after MPFLR but has no cutoff and no predictive validity (N15).
- Validated outcome scores do exist, but they are not an RTS battery. They are the Norwich Patellar Instability score, BPII 2.0, and Kujala, KOOS and IKDC. For Kujala and IKDC, MCID and PASS values exist for MPFLR (Walsh, N16). They measure patient status, not readiness to return.

**Practical points**
- Most patients do not meet the criteria early. Only 14 to 40% met all study-specific criteria (S6). In one adolescent series at about 7.4 months, quadriceps LSI was 85.3% and only 32% passed all four hop tests (N20).
- Average time to RTS is about 6 to 8 months. This is descriptive, not a target. Protocols range from 4 months to 9 to 12 months for contact sport.

**For this app**
The app does not clear a user for sport and does not measure strength LSI, hops or Y-Balance. Final return-to-sport clearance and its criteria stay with the surgeon and care team (see docs/clinical-limits.md). Show any threshold as a limit the care team set, or as a sourced range of practice. Do not present one as a validated pass or fail test.

Sources: research/rehab-rules.md section 6, research/clinical-update.md section 2.4, docs/clinical-limits.md. Most cited studies were checked at abstract level only.
