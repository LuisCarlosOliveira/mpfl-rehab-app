No, do not exercise today. Stop the exercises and contact your care team (surgeon or physiotherapist) today.

Why:
- A knee that is hot and swollen is a warning sign. The app's red-flag list tells you to stop and contact your care team for large or growing swelling, and for infection signs (fever, spreading redness, fluid from the wound, wound pain that grows). Infection after MPFL surgery is uncommon (about 1.3 percent in the research), but heat plus swelling is a reason to have it checked.
- Rehab guidance says to avoid provoking swelling or active inflammation (research/rehab-rules.md, P1). Persistent or recurrent swelling is a reason to notify the surgeon (P2). The evidence is consensus level, and no source gives a number that says when swelling is "too much". So the app does not guess. It asks you to stop and ask a clinician.
- The app cannot tell the cause (overload, infection, a clot or another problem). Only a clinician who examines the knee can. This is general guidance and is not verified for MPFL surgery. It is not a diagnosis.

What to do now:
1. Rest the knee. Skip today's session. Do not "test" it with a few exercises.
2. Call your care team today. Say: the knee is hot and swollen, since when, whether it got worse after exercise, and whether you have a fever or a wound problem.
3. Check yourself for the signs below.

Call emergency services now if:
- you have sudden shortness of breath or chest pain,
- your kneecap moved out of place, or you heard a pop and cannot put weight on the leg,
- your foot is numb, cold or pale, or you cannot feel a pulse in it.

Contact your care team urgently, the same day, if:
- you have a fever, spreading redness, fluid from the wound, or wound pain that grows,
- your calf has pain, swelling, warmth or redness (possible clot),
- the knee gives way, catches or locks, or your knee bend is getting worse.

Go back to exercise only when your care team says it is fine. In the app, answer the red-flag questions honestly. Any "yes" blocks the session. That is intended.
