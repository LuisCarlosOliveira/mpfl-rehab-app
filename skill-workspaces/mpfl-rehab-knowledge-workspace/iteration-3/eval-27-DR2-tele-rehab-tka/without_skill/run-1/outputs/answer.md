No. The project evidence does not show that smart-device tele-rehab beats usual care after total knee arthroplasty (TKA).

The best source in the project knowledge base is a 2025 systematic review of 22 randomised trials (Zhai 2025, listed as T3). It pooled smart-device rehab after TKA against usual care:

- Pain (VAS): SMD 0.02, 95% CI -0.24 to 0.28. This is no difference.
- Function (WOMAC): SMD -0.27, 95% CI -0.60 to 0.06. The interval includes zero, so this is not significant.

So the pooled result is "not different from usual care". It is not "superior". The WOMAC point estimate leans toward a small benefit, but the data do not support that claim.

Limits you must keep in mind:

- The evidence is a review, not a trial of my own. The project marks it REVIEW and BORROWED. The project did not re-check the numbers against the full paper (the citation audit lists T3 as not checked).
- It is TKA evidence. No study of tele-rehab after MPFL reconstruction exists. Do not transfer the result to MPFL patients as proof of benefit or of no benefit.
- A different meta-analysis of 19 RCTs on mixed knee surgery (Wang 2025, J Med Internet Res, N31) reports equal satisfaction and better KOOS (SMD 0.58). Its knee extension value (9.64 degrees) is really "active extension lag" from only 3 trials, and the source is not consistent with itself. The project says not to use that value. It also mixes surgery types and has wide prediction intervals. It does not change the TKA answer above.
- Sword Health (Correia 2018) is not proof of benefit. It was a feasibility study with inertial motion trackers (not a camera) and non-random allocation by geography.

Short answer for the app: tele-rehab after TKA is at best comparable to usual care on pain and function. The app must not claim a benefit from it. Any adjunct such as tele-rehab stays a surgeon decision.
