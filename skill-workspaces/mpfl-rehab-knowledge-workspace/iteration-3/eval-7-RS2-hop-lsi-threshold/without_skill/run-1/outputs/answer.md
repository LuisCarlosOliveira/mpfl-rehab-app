No single hop LSI threshold exists for MPFL reconstruction. The sources disagree, and the figures range from 85 to 95 percent.

Thresholds in the project sources:
- Cincinnati Children's Guideline 46 (2015, P2): hop LSI of 85 percent or more (Tables 3 and 4 exit criteria).
- ISAKOS Sports Medicine Committee 2013 criteria: LSI above 85 percent on hop tests, especially for a return to pivoting sports. I read this second-hand, as printed in Ménétrey 2014. It is a consensus list.
- Hsu et al. 2025 (S1): LSI of 90 percent or more on single-leg hop tests. Phase 3 exit in the same paper uses more than 80 percent for hops. S1 says these criteria are borrowed from ACL practice.
- P3 protocol: two hops above 90 percent.
- Protocols P1 and P4: 95 percent.

Caveats:
- No validated return-to-sport battery exists after MPFL reconstruction (S1: "have not been well established").
- No study links passing hop tests to re-injury after MPFL reconstruction (S6).
- LSI can be inappropriate in bilateral instability (N3, opinion).
- Many patients do not meet the criteria at 6 to 8 months. Only 32 percent passed all 4 hop tests at a mean of 7.4 months (N20, 28 adolescents, case series).

Practical use: the project uses the conservative value of 90 percent or more, from S1 and Ménétrey. Do not average the figures (for example 87.5 percent). Hop tests need space and a clinician, so the app cannot measure them. The clinician enters hop LSI in the app.
