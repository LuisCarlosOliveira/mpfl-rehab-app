No. N31 does not show better knee extension with tele-rehab.

N31 is Wang Y et al., J Med Internet Res 2025;27:e76844. It is a meta-analysis of 19 RCTs of tele-rehabilitation after knee surgery. The project notes first listed "knee extension (MD 9.64 degrees)" as a benefit. That was taken from the abstract. A check against the full text (PMC12716415) corrected it (correction C12 in research/source-audit.md).

- The full text names the 9.64 degree value "active extension lag" (Figure 13, k=3, MD 9.64 degrees, 95% CI 6.89 to 12.39, P = .049, prediction interval 0.60 to 18.68).
- The abstract says "knee extension range". The source does not agree with itself. The value rests on only 3 trials.
- Do not use this number as a benefit of tele-rehab. The wide prediction interval shows that the effect is uncertain.
- The study mixes different knee surgery types.

N31 does report better KOOS (SMD 0.58) and equal satisfaction with tele-rehab. Those findings also come from mixed knee surgery, with wide prediction intervals.

For this app:
- No trial of tele-rehab after MPFL reconstruction exists. All tele-rehab data are borrowed from ACL reconstruction, knee arthroplasty or other knee surgery.
- In the larger review of smart-device tele-rehab after total knee arthroplasty (Zhai 2025, 22 RCTs), pain (VAS SMD 0.02) and function (WOMAC SMD -0.27) did not differ from usual care. Both results were not significant.
- So the app must not claim that tele-rehab gives better knee extension. Say that the data are borrowed and uncertain, and that the surgeon or physiotherapist decides.
