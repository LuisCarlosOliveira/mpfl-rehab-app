No. The app cannot measure quadriceps LSI (limb symmetry index).

Why not:
- Quadriceps LSI compares the strength of the operated leg with the other leg. Studies use isometric or isokinetic peak torque. This needs a dynamometer.
- The app uses a webcam. A webcam cannot measure strength. It can only estimate joint angles and movement.

What the app can do:
- It can compare range of motion and movement symmetry between the two sides. An example is the peak knee angle on a heel slide or mini squat.
- It labels this value "movement symmetry". It never labels it "LSI". It does not call a webcam angle a measurement. (The side-by-side comparison is planned as FR19 and is not built yet.)
- Quadriceps strength stays an item for the clinician. The visit summary lists it as: "Quadriceps strength. Needs a dynamometer. Borrowed from ACL."

Why the number matters:
- Some guidelines use quadriceps LSI as a gate. Examples are more than 70 percent to start running, more than 80 percent to exit phase 3, and 90 percent for return to sport (source S1).
- These limits are borrowed from ACL rehab. Evidence for MPFL reconstruction is weak. Many patients do not reach 90 percent at 6 to 8 months.
- LSI is also not usable when both knees are affected.

Next step: ask your surgeon or physiotherapist to test quadriceps strength with a dynamometer. The app does not replace this test and does not decide when you can return to running or sport.
