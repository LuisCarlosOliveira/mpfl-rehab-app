I cannot give you a single week-by-week table of percent body weight for weeks 0 to 6. The project sources do not contain one, and a made-up table would be unsafe in a protocol table.

What the sources say:

- No consensus exists. The 2023 Delphi of 60 surgeons (N1) found "no consensus on postoperative immobilization or weightbearing". No trial compares weight-bearing schedules after isolated MPFL reconstruction.
- Most published protocols do not use percentages. They state a status, not a body-weight fraction.
  - Weight-bearing as tolerated (WBAT) from day 0 is the common practice. In 35 isolated protocols (S10), 23 (66%) allow immediate WBAT, 9 (26%) allow partial weight-bearing, and 2 (6%) allow non or delayed weight-bearing. R3 reports 65% WBAT.
  - S1 says weight-bearing is usually not limited. The S1 literature range is full weight-bearing from 0 to 8 weeks.
- Only two protocols give a number, and neither covers weeks 0 to 6:
  - P4 (Mass General Brigham, pediatric, 2024): 25 percent body weight with crutches and a locked brace, weeks 0 to 2. It gives no percentage for weeks 3 to 6. P13 copies this template and is not an independent source.
  - P7 (Banff Sport Medicine, 2019): "about half body weight, then full as tolerated". It gives no week numbers.
- Other named protocols: HSS (P1) WBAT with crutches for at least 2 weeks. NHS Lothian (P3) WBAT. Dartmouth (P6) WBAT with crutches for 2 weeks. P12 (combined surgery with lateral lengthening) touchdown weight-bearing for 2 weeks. Cincinnati (P2) says "per surgeon".
- Combined surgery changes this. After a tibial tubercle osteotomy, 56% of protocols use delayed or non weight-bearing. Cartilage work and lateral lengthening also change it. Do not use an isolated-MPFLR row for these patients.
