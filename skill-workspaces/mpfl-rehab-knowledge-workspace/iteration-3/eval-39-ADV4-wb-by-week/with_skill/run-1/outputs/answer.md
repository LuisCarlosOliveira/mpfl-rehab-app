## Disagreements

- S10, R3 and P1, P3 show WBAT as common practice. S1 conclusion asks for gradual progression to full weight-bearing. N1 says there is no consensus.
- The conservative value is partial weight-bearing until the surgeon sets the status (`rehab-rules.md` section 1). This is not a percent. Do not average the sources.
- After a TTO or a cartilage procedure, weight-bearing can be limited. Do not use isolated values. A surgeon protocol is required.

## Single-protocol percentages that exist in the files (not usable for the table)

These come from `research/clinical-update.md` section 3.1. They are single-institution text, and the audit did not check them (rows P4 to P13 were not checked). Treat them as UNVERIFIED and OPINION. They cannot support an app rule.

- P4 (Mass General Brigham, pediatric, 2024): "25 percent with crutches, brace locked, weeks 0 to 2". It gives no value for weeks 3 to 6.
- P7 (Banff Sport Medicine, 2019): "About half body weight, then full as tolerated". It gives no week for the change.
- P1, P3, P5, P6, P8, P10, P11 say WBAT. P2 says per surgeon. P12 (combined surgery) says touchdown for 2 weeks.

These values disagree (25 percent, about 50 percent, WBAT). No week-by-week percent curve exists in any file. A table that I build from them would be invented.

## Ask your surgeon

- Weight-bearing status for each week, and crutch use (`research/clinical.md` section 9, item 2).
- Brace use, because it changes weight-bearing in some protocols.
- Any change for a concurrent procedure.

## Gaps

- NONE: percent of body weight for each week 0 to 6. What the literature says instead: a status word (WBAT, PWB, NWB) and a range of practice, not a percent. S10 gives the counts above.
