There is no proven depth that limits patellofemoral stress after MPFL reconstruction. The project evidence gives a range for healthy people. It does not give a safe limit for a patient with a graft.

What the studies show (all healthy people, no MPFL patients):
- Patellofemoral force and stress in a squat rise as the knee bends. They peak near 90 degrees (Wallace 2002, N=15, S15).
- Wall squat and one-leg squat differ most at 60 to 90 degrees. The authors suggest a knee range of 0 to 50 degrees can limit loading (Escamilla 2009, N=18, S16).
- Squat loads the joint more at 60 to 90 degrees. Knee extension loads it more at 0 to 30 degrees. The curves cross near 48 degrees (Steinkamp 1993, N=20, S14). Powers 2014 (N=10, abstract only) suggests squat from 45 to 0 degrees.
- For lunges, stress rises above about 70 degrees. A long step gives less stress at 70 to 90 degrees than a short step (Escamilla 2008, S17).

So the best-supported shallow range is about 0 to 50 degrees of knee flexion. Use it as a cautious default only. It is borrowed evidence from healthy people. It is not a rule for MPFL patients.

What rehab protocols say after MPFL reconstruction:
- Some protocols give a squat range of 0 to 75 degrees in weeks 9 to 16.
- Another says no closed-chain work past 90 degrees in weeks 6 to 12.
- A guideline goal for the advanced phase is a double-leg squat to 90 degrees with good form, and a single-leg squat to 60 degrees.
These protocols differ. No study measured graft strain or patellofemoral stress during exercise after MPFL reconstruction.

What this means for the app:
- The maximum squat depth and the load limit are a decision for the surgeon or physiotherapist. The project lists this as an item the clinician must set.
- Until the clinician sets a depth, the app can propose 0 to 50 degrees, marked as borrowed evidence.
- Keep the knee from collapsing inward (valgus). No numeric threshold exists for this.
- Stop or reduce depth if pain rises. Combined surgery (for example a bone procedure) needs a different protocol.

This is not medical advice. Confirm any depth with the treating surgeon or physiotherapist.
