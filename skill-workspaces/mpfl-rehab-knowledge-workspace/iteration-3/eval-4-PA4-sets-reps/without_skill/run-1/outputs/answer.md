The evidence gives one weak number for sets and reps. It does not give a dose that the app can set by itself.

**The one source.** Guideline P2 is the Cincinnati Children's Hospital Evidence-Based Care Guideline 46 (2015). It covers lateral patellar dislocation and instability, ages 8 to 25. It excludes realignment surgery. In recommendation 26 it says that strengthening exercises are started in "2 sets of 10-15 repetitions", with progression to "3 sets of each exercise as tolerated". Its note says this applies to all strengthening exercises in all phases. Another note says that if the patient cannot do 2 sets of 10 repetitions, a clinician can lower the intensity.

**How strong it is.** The strength is low. The guideline cites a 2002 strength-training review (Rhea), a 1996 paper on children (Faigenbaum) and a local consensus (2014). These are indirect sources from other groups. No adult study or MPFL reconstruction study sets a dose. The project citation audit calls it opinion with indirect evidence.

**Other dose items in the knowledge base.**
- Intensity, phase 2: S1 gives a target effort (RPE) of 6 to 8 out of 10 for progressive lower-body exercises. This is opinion.
- Intensity, phase 3: S1 says loads above 80 percent of one-repetition maximum may be right, with changes to the set and repetition scheme. It gives no scheme. This is opinion.
- Hold time: P2 gives a straight leg raise without extensor lag for 10 seconds. This is a test goal, not a dose.
- Frequency (sessions per day or week): no source found for exercise after MPFL reconstruction.
- Tempo (seconds per repetition): no source found. The 2 seconds in the app UX notes is a proposal only.
- The heel slide has no dose, hold time or tempo in any source.

**What this means for the app.** The audit verdict is that "2 sets of 10 to 15, up to 3 sets" is safe only as a value that a clinician sets. The app can show it as an example that the clinician confirms. The protocol generator therefore sets no sets or reps. Home shows "How many: ask your care team". The person can enter the numbers that the care team gave (sets 1 to 20, reps 1 to 100), and the app labels them "entered by the person". A test idea in the design is to fail the build if a default of 2 sets appears.

**Short answer.** The best available figure is 2 sets of 10 to 15 repetitions, progressing to 3 sets as tolerated. It comes from a low-strength guideline for a related condition and a younger group. Ask the surgeon or physiotherapist for the actual dose.
