No. The project research found no trial of camera feedback after MPFL reconstruction (MPFLR). It found no study that shows a camera cue improves outcome or safety after knee surgery.

What the research found:

- MPFLR data are small and are not about camera feedback. They are mostly adolescent 3D motion-capture studies (Hsu 2026, 20 patients; Shams 2019, 16 athletes; Samuel 2025, 17 adolescents). They describe movement patterns after surgery. They do not test feedback.
- No MPFLR study gives a validated threshold for knee valgus, trunk lean or pelvic drop. No study links a movement pattern to recurrence.
- Feedback from a therapist is common practice. The MPFLR rehab guideline (P2, rec 30) supports visual, tactile and verbal cues "as needed". This is level 5 consensus. It is not a camera trial.
- The evidence from other conditions or other tools is borrowed and weak:
  - Patellofemoral pain guideline (Willy 2019): Grade B recommendation against visual biofeedback on lower-limb alignment in hip and knee exercises. One high-quality trial found the same gains with and without it. I did not re-read this source (HTTP 403), so treat it as borrowed.
  - Knee and hip osteoarthritis (Biebl 2021): camera form feedback agreed with physiotherapists (kappa 0.551 and 0.626). It showed no outcome benefit.
  - After total knee replacement (TKA), a review of 22 RCTs of smart-device tele-rehab (Zhai 2025) found small, non-significant effects on pain and WOMAC. This is not camera feedback after MPFLR.
  - Sword digital biofeedback after TKA used inertial sensors, not a camera. It did not randomize.
- The 2D camera is also limited. The best-case knee angle error is about 9 degrees (video can reach 10 to 22 degrees). A goniometer after knee surgery has about 8 degrees of error. Frontal-plane (valgus) angles are not valid in 2D. No study tested the MediaPipe `lite` model that the plan uses.

What this means for the app: a camera cue is an app behavior that needs clinician approval. It is not an evidence-based treatment. The research supports these uses only:

- Patient cues against a limit that a clinician sets, with an error margin. The cues are knee-bend ceiling and target (side view), the open-chain arc end, rep time and leg visibility.
- A clinician log (no patient message) for the straight leg raise top angle, trunk lean and movement symmetry.
- No "good form" or "bad form" verdict, and no live "fix your knee" cue for alignment.

Sources: `research/form-evidence.md`, `research/form-opus.md`, `research/form.md`. The search for a trial covered what these files record. A new trial published after 2026-11-08 would not appear here.
