I will not apply the isolated MPFLR plan to a trochleoplasty patient. The app must refuse this and send the patient to the surgeon.

Reasons

1. The evidence base is missing. A review of rehab protocols (S10) found that "isolated trochleoplasty protocols were too few to support a representative rehabilitation pathway". The knowledge base has no protocol for trochleoplasty that the app can use. The one source that gives a trochleoplasty plan (UVA: MPFL with TTO and trochleoplasty) is UNVERIFIED, and its PDF returns 404.
2. The outcomes differ. S23 (Pozzi 2026, LoE IV, about 23 patients) reports that after trochleoplasty plus MPFLR, quadriceps and hamstring strength stayed reduced at 2 years. Patellofemoral cartilage worsened in 18 of 24 knees. The evidence is low-level, but it shows that this surgery is not the same as isolated MPFLR.
3. Combined surgery needs a more protective approach. P1 says "Follow most conservative guideline" for concomitant procedures. S1 says weight-bearing changes with a concurrent procedure. P2 excludes bone realignment. R5 found that all papers on bony realignment lacked adequate return-to-play guidelines. S10 shows protective practice for the related TTO case (delayed or no weight-bearing in 56 percent, locked brace more than 6 weeks in 67 percent). No dose data exist for trochleoplasty.
4. The risk is overload. The isolated plan sets phases, weight-bearing, range of motion, brace and return-to-sport steps from isolated MPFLR data. Applied to combined surgery, it can overload the knee.

What the app does

- Intake question I2 ("Which operation did you have?") accepts only "MPFL reconstruction only". If the patient picks "groove reshaping (trochleoplasty)", the app shows referral R2: "The app makes plans only after MPFL reconstruction with no other procedure. Contact your care team for your plan."
- The app does not generate a plan, a phase, an exercise list or a return-to-sport timeline for this patient. Choosing "I do not know" is handled the same way.
- Weight-bearing, brace, range-of-motion limits, squat depth, load limits, phase changes, running and RTS clearance are surgeon decisions. The knowledge base lists "any change in protocol for concurrent surgery: TTO, trochleoplasty, cartilage repair" as a surgeon decision.
- The surgeon must give the written protocol. The app can load that protocol only as a file or entry that the person confirms, and the app does not write it. Any use of a surgeon protocol for this surgery needs the surgeon's approval.

What I can offer instead

- General information on why combined surgery is handled differently (the evidence above).
- Help with questions to ask the surgeon: weight-bearing status, brace use and end date, range-of-motion limits, strengthening start, and criteria for return to sport.
- The standard red-flag advice (a new giving-way feeling, a visible patella shift, sudden swelling, calf pain or swelling, sudden breathlessness) is general practice and UNVERIFIED as a source. The patient must report any of these to the surgeon or a physician.

Limits of this answer

The sources I used are abstracts and full texts listed in `research/clinical.md`, `research/rehab-rules.md`, `research/specialists-brief.md` (SR5) and `docs/protocol-generator-design.md` (I2, R2). The trochleoplasty evidence is small (S23 is retrospective, about 23 patients). The refusal rests on the lack of any usable protocol and on the consensus to follow the most conservative guideline, not on proof that a specific isolated-plan step is harmful.
